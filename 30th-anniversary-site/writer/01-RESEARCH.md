## Founding & Origin Story

*Source: "Uncommon brand story and timeline" (Maria Alcon-Heraux, internal doc — canonical history narrative).*

**The landscape.** Through the 1980s and 1990s, urban school districts were in crisis — declining and stagnant academic performance, concentrated in underserved communities. In 1995, the New Jersey legislature passed the bipartisan Charter School Program Act, opening the door for independently operated public charter schools.

**The founders.** Norman Atkins — a social activist whose anti-poverty work at New York's Robin Hood Foundation convinced him to intervene early enough in a child's life that they never fell behind — took his dream to Newark, where he partnered with visionary social studies teacher Jamey Verrilli.

**The first school.** In **1997**, with the support of local families, Atkins and Verrilli opened **North Star Academy** — a single floor of fifth- and sixth-grade classrooms serving **72 Newark students**. Dedicated parents chose the name as a nod to African-American history: *The North Star* was Frederick Douglass's abolitionist newspaper, and the star itself guided enslaved people to freedom. The school was built on the belief that a community-centered, college-preparatory program could dramatically change learning and life outcomes.

**The movement.** NSA's co-founders collaborated with the charter pioneers of the era: Doug Lemov co-founded Academy of the Pacific Rim in Boston (1997); Brett Peiser launched South Boston Harbor Academy (1998); John King, Evan Rudall, and J. Keith Motley founded Roxbury Prep (1999). These leaders met regularly to share and iterate best practices — and their schools outperformed their districts.

**The network.** In 2004 Atkins founded Excellence Boys in Brooklyn. In **2005**, North Star (Newark), True North (Rochester, led by Lemov), Collegiate (Brooklyn, led by Peiser), and Excellence Schools (Brooklyn) united under the **Uncommon Schools** umbrella as a charter management organization, with a new Home Office supporting the schools. That same year, North Star graduated its first high school class — 19 students, **100% college acceptance**.

**Founder afterlives (credibility anchors):** Norman Atkins co-founded Relay Graduate School of Education. John King became U.S. Secretary of Education (Obama administration), now SUNY Chancellor. Doug Lemov wrote *Teach Like a Champion*; Paul Bambrick-Santoyo wrote *Driven by Data* and *Get Better Faster*. Brett Peiser and founding teacher Julie Jackson serve as Co-CEOs today. Jamey Verrilli founded the Hollyhock Humanities Fellowship.

**Anniversary math (important):** The 30 years count from North Star Academy's 1997 founding. The campaign runs across SY25-26 and SY26-27, closing at the true 30-year mark in 2027. Never claim "founded 1995" — 1995 is the NJ charter law, not the school.

### Achievement & context stats
- **86%** of students are economically disadvantaged; **66%** are first-generation college students
- **1,000+ seniors** head to college every year; alumni graduate college at **4x** the rate of low-income peers (54% vs. 16% nationally)
- Class of 2025: **53%** passed at least one AP exam — ahead of national and NJ/NY/MA state averages
- As of Fall 2025: **25% (1,742 of 7,053)** of alumni accepted to or enrolled at the nation's most selective colleges
- Schools outperform local districts across all regions; in several cases exceed state averages and non-economically-disadvantaged proficiency rates
- The need, then and now: 1997 — only 6% of Black and Latino students proficient in 4th-grade reading; today — only 13% proficient in 8th-grade math; average low-income 8th grader reads at 4th-grade level; low-income students drop out at 5x the rate of high-income peers
- Top 3% of US school districts by scale; highest-performing large charter network (Stanford CREDO)
