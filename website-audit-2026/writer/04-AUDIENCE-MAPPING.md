# 04 — Audience Mapping
**Phase 01: Content Audit | Uncommon Schools**

> For each of the three core audiences — Families, Educators, and Donors — what content currently serves them, what's missing, and what's buried that should be front-and-center.

## Families / Students

**Goal:** Understand why Uncommon is the right choice → find their nearest school → enroll

### What's Working
- The Student Experience page is specific and trust-building — it describes what school actually feels like
- Results data is strong (96% college acceptance, Stanford CREDO reference)
- FAQ answers basic charter school questions

### What's Missing
- **School-specific content** — families can't evaluate schools from the main site. Every school page is a 200-word placeholder from 2018.
- **Clear enrollment pathway** — currently 3–4 clicks from the homepage to find a city-specific enrollment form. Should be 1.
- **Family FAQ** — "What is a charter school lottery?" "What does a school day look like?" "Is it really free?" These are the real questions. They exist in fragments; they need to be consolidated and schema-marked.
- **Grade-level guidance** — a parent of a 5th grader has completely different questions than a parent of a 9th grader. The site treats all families identically.
- **Spanish-language content** — 95% of Uncommon students are Black or Latino. The site is English-only. This is an accessibility and equity gap, not just a content gap.

### What's Buried (Should Be Front-and-Center)
- **Enroll CTA** — currently nested under "Our Schools" in the nav. Should be a primary nav item or a hero CTA on the homepage.
- **Individual school pages** — the homepage signals that 52 schools exist but gives families no real way to evaluate them.

## Staff / Educators (Prospective + Current)

**Goal (prospective):** Understand why Uncommon is a great place to work → explore open roles → apply
**Goal (current/external):** Access professional development resources, curriculum, blog

### What's Working
- Careers section has a decent structure
- Sharing Our Practices is a genuine differentiator — no competitor offers this
- HS Curriculum Hub is a real tool resource for educators nationally
- Uncommon Sense blog is active and practitioner-relevant

### What's Missing
- **A flagship "Why Uncommon?" page** — currently a dropdown item. Should be a full narrative page about professional growth, coaching culture, and educator identity at Uncommon. The answer to "why would I leave my current school for this?"
- **Educator-specific nav pathway** — prospective teachers navigate the same nav structure as families enrolling their kids. These are completely different users with completely different needs.
- **Thought leadership → recruitment connection** — if you love Teach Like a Champion, the site gives you no path to "work with the people who wrote it." This bridge doesn't exist.
- **Staff testimonials with substance** — not one-sentence pull quotes. Real stories about professional growth, coaching, career trajectory.
- **External PD workshop info** — Sharing Our Practices mentions external educator programs but they're not developed into bookable content.

## Donors

**Goal:** Understand the impact of their gift → trust the organization → give

### What's Working
- Donate page has tiered giving with concrete impact statements ($100 = class software access for a semester, etc.)
- Results data provides proof of mission delivery
- About Us history and milestones establish organizational credibility

### What's Missing
- **Alumni Impact page** — this is the most important page for donor storytelling. Stories of students who graduated college because of Uncommon. The page 404s. This is a primary navigation item that goes nowhere.
- **Annual / impact report** — no equivalent anywhere on the site
- **Major donor pathway** — no foundations/grants section, no named giving opportunities, no "ways to give at scale"
- **Donor FAQ** — How is Uncommon funded? What percentage goes to classrooms? What is the annual budget? These questions create trust hesitation that good FAQ content resolves.
- **Narrative giving data** — "In 2024, donor gifts funded 847 students through the college application process" is more powerful than a static $1,000 tier. The infrastructure exists (tiered giving copy); the story doesn't.
